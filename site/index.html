Both sources at once, in points: housing 186 × 32, panel 520 × 146, opened with the app's own spring. Hover the island, drag the playbar, switch tabs with the toggle. The waveform is a live FFT of audio synthesised in your browser — original, arranged to build and drop, so give it fifteen seconds.

What it does

Two live activities, one strip of dead space.

The housing is 186 × 32 points. Everything had to earn its place in that band, or in the 520 × 146 panel that unfolds from it.

Music

Now playing, and the real signal

Album art and transport for Spotify, with a waveform driven by a Core Audio process tap on it — not a timer, and not anything else playing on the system.

Claude Code

Opens when Claude needs you

The session's state rides on the other side of the camera. An approval opens the panel on its own, so you don't have to be watching the terminal.

Both

Out of the way by default

A borderless, non-activating panel with no Dock icon. Idle, it shrinks to exactly the camera housing and disappears into the hardware.

The thing that costs everyone a day

Since roughly macOS 15.4, MRMediaRemoteGetNowPlayingInfo returns an empty dictionary to unentitled apps rather than failing — so an app built the textbook way silently never shows a track, and nothing in the logs says why. Verified through macOS 26.5.

Install

Two permissions, then it's out of your way.

A private framework and AppleScript automation rule out the Mac App Store, so Isle ships as a disk image and updates itself from there. First launch is right-click → Open. macOS then asks twice: Automation, to control Spotify, and audio capture, for the waveform — decline the second and the waveform falls back to a procedural pattern rather than going dead.
